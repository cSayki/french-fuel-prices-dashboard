# Demo video script (about 3 and a half minutes)

Open the app with `streamlit run app.py` and put the browser in full width. On a Mac, Cmd + Shift
+ 5 lets you record the whole screen. The text below is what to say; the lines in brackets are
what to do on screen.

## Introduction (0:00 to 0:25)

"In September 2026, a litre of diesel cost 2.40 euros on average in France, more than at the 2022
peak. Most people blame the price of oil. I wanted to check that, using the 36 million prices that
French stations have declared since 2019 and the monthly price of Brent."

(Show the four figures at the top, then scroll to the audience and the questions.)

## Overview (0:25 to 1:00)

"The figures at the top follow the filters in the sidebar. Here is diesel. You can see the low
point in May 2020 during the lockdown, the spike in 2022 after the invasion of Ukraine, with the
State rebate in the shaded area, and the new jump from March 2026 with the war in Iran and the
Strait of Hormuz."

(Hover over the line. Switch the measure to the index and back. Point at the stock-out bars.)

"And a shock is not only about prices. In October 2022 the refinery strikes left thousands of
stations empty."

## Oil and the pump price (1:00 to 1:50)

"So what is a litre made of? Crude oil is only about a quarter. Taxes are more than half, because
the excise is a fixed amount per litre and VAT is added on top. And the orange layer, refining,
transport and retail, went from about 23 to 56 cents per litre for diesel."

(Scroll to the scatter plot.)

"People often say prices go up like a rocket and come down like a feather. Increases are passed on
at 1.6 and decreases at 0.85, but the confidence intervals overlap, so with monthly data I can't
really prove it, and I prefer to say so."

## Regions (1:50 to 2:40)

"The map shows each department compared with the national average."

(Hover over Bas-Rhin and Corsica.)

"The small charts compare each region on the same scale. Brittany is cheaper almost all the time,
and Corsica moves a lot because it has its own tax rates."

(Set the station type to Motorway, then back to All.)

"On the motorway you pay about 9 to 15 cents more per litre."

(Zoom on your own city on the station map.)

## Scenarios (2:40 to 3:10)

"Here you can test your own scenario."

(Move Brent to 120 euros, then lower the excise by 10 cents.)

"Roughly, 10 euros more on a barrel adds 7.5 cents per litre, so about 60 euros a year for an
average driver."

## Data quality and conclusion (3:10 to 3:30)

"The data quality tab explains each cleaning step. Only 0.14 % of the rows were removed, and it
lists the limits, for example that the averages count stations and not litres sold. To sum up, oil
explains why prices move, taxes explain most of the level, and margins explain the trend. Thank
you."
